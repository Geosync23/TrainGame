What gets built, in three stages

Each stage ends tested, saved and playable. If the budget runs out, the finished stages stand.

Stage 1: the line and the crew (tonight)

  • The country: a long strip running from the station to a far town, wide enough that from the track you never see its edge. Prairie, hills, woods, rock, a river, with brush to clear, a creek to bridge and a low rise to cut through.
  • The work train: leaves just before the first passenger train with the crew and the day's supplies, stands at the end of track, and brings everyone home in the evening.
  • The crew: 5 to 30 men, each simulated on his own: he picks up a tool, walks to his place, works, tires, rests, eats at noon. Six stages follow one another down the line: clear, dig the bed, lay the rock, lay the beams, lay the rails, spike them down.
  • Pace: a full crew of 30 reaches the far town in about 14 working days; 5 men take about six times as long. Track laid is kept from day to day and drawn on the map.
  • The camp: tents by the town with one bed per rail worker, growing with the crew. Every man is counted home at night.
  • Dev mode: a panel to set the crew size, hire station workers, and "ride out as a rail worker". For now you ride out and watch, and can walk among the men.
  • The longer day: the same hours on a slower clock, about one and a half times as long to play.

Stage 2: joining in, and hired station workers

  • A small game per stage: swing in time to dig and to spike, carry and place beams and rails with a partner, tip and rake the rock, cut brush. You count as one good worker and lift the men near you. You can step out at any time and watch.
  • Hired workers at the station: ticket punchers, cargo loaders and delivery men who do their jobs in view and take whatever you leave. While you are out on the line, anything nobody covers goes undone and counts against you.
  • Lessons for line building, in the same style as the others.

Stage 3: the bigger town

  • Houses for everyone, and every building can be entered: the roof and front wall fade and you see the room.
  • A role for each person (keeper, smith, clerk, teacher, hand and so on), with a home, a workplace and a daily round.
  • A simple, visible economy: delivered goods keep shops stocked and open; stocked shops mean work and money for their people; missed goods mean shut doors and idle, unhappy people; travellers bring custom to the hotel and saloon.
  • The far town drawn at the end of the line (not yet playable).
