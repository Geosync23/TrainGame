# Prototype (D25): the sprite contract

The agreement between the pixel renderer (builder R, in `index.html`) and the art pipeline (`tools/pixelart/`).
Written by the D25 architect; the full technical plan is `plans/proto-tech.md`. If something here does not suit the
art, say so in `tools/pixelart/NOTES.md` and the integrator settles it; do not quietly change a name or an anchor.

Everything is **provisional for the designer** where it is a look decision (sizes, colours, which people exist).

## 1. The projection (fixed: `PIX_PROJ` in index.html)

Native pixels, before the whole-number upscale:

- world **+x** (along the main line, west to east) moves **8 px right and 4 px down**;
- world **+z** (south, towards the viewer) moves **8 px left and 4 px down**;
- world **+y** (up) moves **8 px up**.

So a native pixel position is `sx = OX + 8 (x - z)`, `sy = OY + 4 (x + z) - 8 y`, rounded to whole pixels
(`Math.round`) only at the end. The main line runs from the upper left to the lower right of the screen, as in mockup
2. North (negative z: the station, the street and the shops) is up and to the right; south (positive z) is down and to
the left, nearest the viewer. The light is the mockup's: tops lightest, the face turned to the viewer's left (the +z
face) lit, the face to the right (the +x face) in shade.

`PIX_PROJ` (index.html, section "pixel renderer (D25)"): `{ PX: 8, PY: 4, PH: 8, OX, OY, W, H, DECK: 3 }`. `OX`, `OY`
place world (0, 0, 0) in the ground layer image; `W`, `H` are that image's size; `DECK` is the drawn height of a
platform deck in px (people and goods on a deck are drawn 3 px higher; the simulation stays at height 0).
The layout's exact numbers (track centre lines sampled every 0.25u, switches, platforms, buildings, the walkway) are
exported to `tools/pixelart/proto-layout.json` (`node tools/pixelart/export-layout.js`), so the pipeline can bake the
ground layer and the buildings exactly where the game has them. Re-export after any layout change.

Scale: 1u is 8 px across the screen per unit of x. People are drawn bigger than their 3D size: **an adult is about
20 px from heel to crown (2.5u; a top hat adds 1 px), a child about 14 px** (people v2: the designer signed off the
sample on 2026-11-11 and, after a 26 px trial the same day, chose "about 20, the size of the sample you saw"). They
were 14 px (1.75u, the mockup's) before; that set is parked in `tools/pixelart/people_v1.py`. The renderer sorts a
person as a box `PIX.PERSON_H` (2.5u) tall. The
prototype's trains are drawn bigger than the 3D game's (see the sizes below) so they keep the
mockup's proportions.

## 2. Sheet format

- One atlas PNG (or a few), indexed colour or RGBA with a hard alpha (0 or 255, no soft edges), no anti-aliasing.
- Delivered as a generated JavaScript block `tools/pixelart/out/atlas.js`:
  `const PIX_ATLAS = { v: 1, sheets: ['data:image/png;base64,...'], frames: { '<key>': [sheet, x, y, w, h, ax, ay], ... } };`
  `ax, ay`: the **anchor** inside the frame (pixels from the frame's top left), the pixel where the thing's world anchor
  point lands. The renderer draws a frame at `(sx - ax, sy - ay)`.
- R pastes it between the markers `// ---- PIX_ATLAS begin (generated by tools/pixelart, do not edit) ----` and
  `// ---- PIX_ATLAS end ----` in index.html. Budget (D27, plans/d27-tech.md 7.4): the whole atlas under 700 KB of base64, target 600 (was 500); no baked ground layer (the browser composes the ground from `tile/` sprites; `build.py --ground` still bakes the D25 one).
- Missing keys are drawn by the renderer as flat placeholder shapes (what the foundation draws today), so art can land
  piece by piece.
- Mirroring: keys marked "(mirrored)" below are drawn by the renderer flipped left to right for the other facing; the
  art provides only the facing named.

## 3. Directions

- People and the handcart: four facings, the screen diagonals. `se` = walking +x (down right), `ne` = walking -z (up
  right), `sw` = +z (down left), `nw` = -x (up left). **The art draws `se` (front, three quarters) and `ne` (back, three
  quarters); the renderer mirrors `se` for `sw` and `ne` for `nw`.** People are the exception (the lead, 2026-11-10):
  all four facings are drawn and lit from the upper left, and the renderer never mirrors them.
- Vehicles: by heading, `yaw` as the game measures it (radians, `atan2(-dz, dx)`: 0 is east, +x; positive turns north).
  Ten headings: `e`, `e+15`, `e+30`, `e-15`, `e-30` (east, and 15 or 30 degrees north (+) or south (-) of it, for the
  crossovers) and `w`, `w+15`, `w+30`, `w-15`, `w-30` (west: yaw 180 degrees, +15 turning north of west ...). The
  renderer takes the nearest of the ten. No mirroring (the far end of a loco differs from its front).
- Switch stands: one facing (seen from the viewer).

### The ground layer (`ground`)

One image `W x H` (PIX_PROJ) with world (0, 0, 0) at (OX, OY): grass, the street, ballast, ties and rails of every
track (switches included, drawn with their points closed for the straight route; `sw/points/...` overlays show the
state), platform decks with their edges (at DECK px), the plank walkway, the trodden paths. Nothing that stands up
higher than a deck (buildings, stands, lamps) is in it. Until it exists, R draws the ground procedurally.
