M1 fix round, plan for your review

Railhead M1 fix round

Your end-of-M1 review asked for a bigger game, a readable platform, a view into the cars, crossings, and a departure board in the world. This page says what I found, what I plan to build, and asks the questions only you can answer. Nothing is built until you agree. Your answers save on this page as you go.

What you asked for

  • Scale the whole game up: the map, the trains, the platform ("way too small") and everything else.
  • The platform roof hides the platform.
  • Platform clipping: the player's feet sink into the platform and the freight dock.
  • A cutaway view into the cars when you board, with you shown inside.
  • Crossings: track is no longer walkable everywhere.
  • The departure board in the world as well as the card.

Then you retest. The playtest with outside players comes later.

What I found

A unit (u) in the game is about one person's height, 1.75 m. Next to a person, the trains and cars are about half the size of real 1866 ones. That is the main reason the trains and the platform look small.

Making everything bigger by the same amount, people included, looks exactly like zooming in, so on its own it would not make the trains look bigger next to you. That is why the first question asks what "bigger" should mean.

Your feet sink because the game always draws you at ground height, while the platform and dock floors are 0.3u up. Travellers on the platform are already drawn on top.

The plan

The round is built in pieces. The sizes below follow my suggested answers; they change with yours.

1. Scale

Trains, cars, track, platform, buildings and distances grow 1.6 times; people stay their size and the camera starts a bit closer. The map grows, with more open land around the hub. Train speeds grow to match, so the timetable, fares and wood use stay as they are. Your saved game is converted to the new sizes when you open it; if a save cannot be converted it is kept, not lost, and a new game starts with a note. This is the biggest piece: it touches nearly every part of the game, and all 28 checks have positions written into them, so every check is updated and the test player's week must still pass.

2. Feet on the floor

You stand on the platform and the dock and step up and down at their edges.

3. The platform roof

It turns see-through when you are under it or near it (or whatever you choose below).

4. Crossings

Track becomes something you walk around, not over. Plank crossings at the stations; tapping across the track walks you round by the nearest crossing. You still work switches, couplings, wood and water from beside the track. A train standing on a crossing blocks it. Building track by hand is unchanged: you can stand on your own unfinished track.

5. Seeing into the cars

When you board, that car opens up: roof and near wall hidden, you and the travellers shown inside. It closes when you step off. The open side follows the camera when you turn it.

6. Departure board

A painted board in the world showing the next departures, readable when zoomed in. The card stays.

7. Small gaps (if you want them)

Picking materials back up at a building job; a switch inside a station's stopping track no longer cancels its trains; a warning mark on an item in the wrong bay.

How it is built and tested

  • Helpers inside this session do the building. Scale goes first, by one helper, because everything else sits on top of it. Then pieces 2 to 7 by up to three helpers side by side.
  • Every piece gets its own check (scale and old saves, feet, roof, crossings, cutaway, board). All checks pass before anything is saved to GitHub.
  • Phone speed: the busy scene uses 73 draw calls now (budget 100). The roof, the open car and the board add about 5 to 8. The bigger map is measured after piece 1; if the walking route search gets slow, I make it coarser away from the stations.
  • One thing to watch: the test for "two trains within 10 minutes" passes at exactly 10 minutes, so new speeds could tip it. If they do I tell you rather than nudge the timetable quietly.
  • At the end: a new playable link and a short review page for your retest.
