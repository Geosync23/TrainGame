Milestone 2 plan, for your review

M2: the living town

Goal from the design doc: towns grow or stall visibly because of what you deliver. The gate at the end: a town visibly grows or stalls from what you deliver. Nothing is built until you agree.

What M2 adds

  • Named citizens who arrive on the Overland, buy a ticket and ride your train to their town, with a home, a job, a daily routine and a pantry.
  • Needs and satisfaction: households use up flour, coffee and other goods each week. When the shelves run empty they grow unhappy, and in the end they pack up and leave on a train.
  • A developer who builds houses from the lumber and nails you deliver, with builders carrying the materials, and a new household moving in.
  • Real businesses (the settlement and village ones in section 12) replace M1's placeholder stores, with staff, opening hours and prospects ("a tailor will open if fabric comes weekly").
  • Ways to read the town: tap any person for their card (name, home, job, the train they came on), a ledger for each town, a weekly newspaper, and signs you can see: empty windows, tents, queues.

Proposed order: four rounds, each played and reviewed by you

RoundWhat you can see at the end
A. PeopleNamed citizens step off the Overland, ride to Willow Ford, live in houses, walk to work and home. Tap anyone for their card.
B. NeedsHouseholds use up goods. Stop delivering flour to Willow Ford and within 10 game days people grow unhappy, one family leaves on your train, and the newspaper says so.
C. BuildingDeliver lumber and nails, and the developer builds a house you watch go up. A newly arrived family moves in.
D. Businesses and readingReal businesses with workers and prospects, a ledger for each town, and visible signs of trouble. Then the M2 gate.

Each round: a short plan, builders working in parallel (about 4 to 5 helpers), the full tests, a playable link and a review survey. Phones stay first, and the busiest scene stays under 100 draw calls.
