End of M1, the whistle-stop

Railhead M1 review

All of M1 is built: you work the hub and Willow Ford alone, by hand. This page shows what it does now, how the test week went, what is still missing, and the choices the builders made that the design doc did not settle. Your answers save on this page as you go.

Play the M1 build

What M1 does now

  • A real start. A new game opens at the hub on Monday 05:00, spring 1866, with $2,500, engine No. 1, a combine and a boxcar, and a 10-mile spur east to Willow Ford.
  • The investor's tutorial. Telegrams from E. Hartwell walk you through days 1 and 2: take a contract, order the goods, throw a switch, sell tickets, unload, sort and deliver. It can be skipped.
  • Freight as real items. Every crate, sack and bundle has a waybill and a tag. You carry them by hand, hand truck or handcart, sort them into the freight shed's bays and take them to the business doors.
  • Money and orders. Order goods at the telegraph key; they come on the Overland. Businesses offer contracts; a ledger keeps every cent, checked every day.
  • Placeholder towns. The hub's and Willow Ford's businesses use up stock each week and ask for more.
  • Passengers. Travellers queue at the ticket window, board, ride and step off. At Willow Ford, with nobody selling tickets, they pay on arrival.
  • The timetable. No. 1 runs to Willow Ford twice a day once you turn it on. You take water, load cordwood onto the tender and let it run round at Willow Ford.
  • Riding your own train between the stations, instead of a 93-minute walk.
  • Building track by hand. The Build tool plans; a planned job is staked with string. You set ties, drop rails and drive every spike, using materials delivered by train.
  • The office. At the depot desk: Contracts, Market, Ledger, Trains and Timetable. Telegrams slide in under the clock; a Look button shows any thing's card.
  • Saving. Progress saves in the browser; every older save still loads.

The M1 test results

The design doc sets four tests for M1. A test player (a small program that plays through the game's own buttons, nothing else) did the first three.

Passed

Finish the tutorial with no debug tools

The test player finished every step within days 1 and 2. Skipping it and saving halfway also work.

Passed

Play a full week alone

Imports arrived, were sorted and delivered, No. 1 kept time, and the money and item checks passed at the end of every day. Figures below.

Passed

Two trains within 10 minutes make pressure

On six evenings the Overland and No. 1 came in 10 or 11 minutes apart, with a ticket queue, an unsorted pile and a train waiting at the same moment. On day 1: 4 in the queue, 38 items unsorted. This passes at exactly 10 minutes, so a timing change could tip it.

Known gaps

One person cannot keep two ticket windows. 283 travellers gave up in the week. Paying on arrival helps at Willow Ford; at the hub they still give up while you are away. A station agent (later) is the planned fix.

The week only breaks even. The test player ended $0.52 up. It plays carefully but not cleverly, so this says more about the bot than the economy, but it is worth a look in playtesting.

You can walk across track anywhere. The design doc says only at crossings, but crossings are not built yet.

Your feet sink into the platform and the dock. The player is always drawn at ground height.

Materials put down at a building job cannot be picked back up; only cancelling the job returns them.

A switch built inside a station's stopping track cancels that station's timetabled runs.

Not built yet: a warning mark on an item sorted into the wrong bay; crossover and ladder track shapes; fetching materials from the pile while building.

Hand-building is quicker than you asked: a 20u siding takes about 3 real minutes at 1x, not 5. Your step times (tie 0.5, rail pair 1, spike 1.3 game minutes) add up to that, so they were kept as you set them. One of the questions below asks about it.

Test-machine wobbles: two checks needed longer waits because the test machine draws slowly. Not a game problem.
