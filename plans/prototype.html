Streamlined prototype

You asked to back up: one small station, two platforms close together, a day of passenger and freight trains, switches, punching tickets, unloading and loading freight, and at the end of the day sorting the goods into a cart and delivering them to a few shops. Everything else is hidden for now, not deleted. Up to 5 helpers. A few choices below decide how they build it.

A day in the prototype

  • Morning. The day opens at the schedule board: every inbound and outbound train for the day, passenger and freight, with times.
  • A freight train comes in from the end of the line. You throw the switch so it runs onto the freight platform, then start unloading it into storage. Freight trains stand a long time.
  • A passenger train is due. Travellers are already lined up on the passenger platform, waiting to board, so there is no ticket booth. You throw the switch for it, it pulls in, travellers get off, you punch tickets as the line boards, set the switch for its way out (before or after) and send it off. Passenger trains come and go quickly.
  • Back to the freight: finish unloading into storage, set its switch, send it off.
  • More trains through the day; some freight trains bring goods, some take goods away (you load them from storage).
  • Evening. Sort the day's goods by shop into a handcart and wheel it to the shops in town to deliver them.
  • Hidden for now: the work board and contracts, ordering by telegraph, money and prices, building, the investor's telegrams, wood and water, No. 1, Willow Ford, the Overland, the bigger hub. They stay in the game file, switched off, for later.

How the helpers would split it

  • One helper first lays out the station and the day's schedule, and switches the rest off.
  • Then three at once: the trains and switches; the travellers and ticket punching; the freight, storage, handcart and shops.
  • One more does the screens (schedule board, a simple "next" line, buttons), joins the pieces, and a robot player plays the day to test it.

Pixel graphics: my answer

  • A pixel look on what we have is small work: draw the 3D scene at a low resolution and blow it up with sharp square pixels, with a smaller set of colours and dark outlines. A few days at most, and nothing else in the game changes. It would look like chunky pixel art, but the shapes would still be the 3D ones.
  • True hand-drawn pixel art is a lot of work: every building, train car, person and their walking frames drawn as pictures, from each angle the camera can turn to, and a new way of drawing the whole world. That is close to a new game's worth of art.
  • My suggestion: after the prototype plays well, try the pixel look as a quick test on a copy, and you decide from screenshots. Nothing is done until you say so.
